## 1. Purpose & Editorial Boundaries

This document provides the complete analytical breakdown and editorial specification accompanying `ANALYTICAL_EXPLAINER_BIO` and `ANALYTICAL_EXPLAINER_DNA` in `/creators/dhruv.ts`.

### Key Boundaries
- **Genre-Level Framework**: Captures broad documentary craft choices visible across the supplied transcripts—curiosity-first hooks, chronological reconstruction, accessible mechanisms, contrast, source-aware storytelling, and context-led promotion.
- **Original Wording & Verification**: Do not imitate an identifiable living person's exact personal voice, distinctive phrasing, or delivery quirks, or claim to be that person. Treat transcript samples as structural writing samples and verify every factual claim independently.
- **Editorial Source-Cadence Safeguards**: The source-placement targets below are recommended editorial policy safeguards, not measurements of how frequently the original videos show source cards or speak citations aloud.
- **Strict Field Separation**: Keep spoken narration, visual direction, audio direction, and source-ledger citations in separate fields so production notes never leak into voiceover.

## 3. Sample-by-Sample Transcript Breakdown (`transcriptAnalysis`)

### Sample 1: `paranormal_mystery`
- **Observed Hook**: A familiar film shoot and a named actor's reported encounter establish an eerie scene before expanding to the broader question of ghosts.
- **Hook Mechanism**:
  - Start with a concrete time, place, or incident.
  - Use the setting and reported experience to create curiosity, not proof.
  - Expand from one story to many similar claims, then ask what explains them.
- **Body Mechanism**:
  - First remove cases presented as obvious frauds.
  - Use named examples to show how tricks, incentives, or media repetition can spread a claim.
  - Then examine harder cases and consider psychological or social explanations rather than assuming deliberate lying in every case.
- **Source Observation**:
  - ABC News is named at about `3:56`, an Instagram user is described at about `4:39`, and later reporting is mentioned around `6:50`. Many earlier claims have no explicit spoken citation in the excerpt.
- **Core Learning**:
  - Use a mystery hook, but make the investigation more nuanced than a binary "believers are right / believers are foolish" argument. Attribute eyewitness accounts and distinguish a report from proof.

### Sample 2: `investigative_accountability`
- **Observed Hook**: A message received on a specific date claims to predict an exam paper; the dramatic question-match figure then raises stakes for a very large student population.
- **Hook Mechanism**:
  - Open inside a chronological scene rather than introducing the topic abstractly.
  - Place a concrete piece of evidence beside a high-stakes consequence.
  - Use one strong number to create a question, then explain its denominator and verification.
- **Body Mechanism**:
  - Trace the alleged chain from production to distribution.
  - Use dates, named roles, payments, and hand-offs as chronological waypoints, if each is supported.
  - Follow the institutional response, conflict between public statements, and human consequences.
  - End with accountability, limitations, and realistic next steps.
- **Source Observation**:
  - *Indian Express* is named around `3:49` and *India Today* around `7:42` in the supplied transcript. Institutional names such as NTA, CBI, and police also appear, but naming an institution is not the same as citing a specific underlying document.
- **Core Learning**:
  - An investigative timeline feels compelling when each hand-off is verifiable. Never let names, political associations, or suspicious coincidences stand in for proof of involvement.

### Sample 3: `ai_culture_creativity`
- **Observed Hook**: A montage of AI capabilities leads to a philosophical question about whether automation threatens art and human creativity.
- **Hook Mechanism**:
  - Show several immediately recognisable capabilities before explaining them.
  - Turn the demonstration into a dilemma with consequences for people.
  - Set up an argument that can be explored through examples rather than a binary yes/no answer.
- **Body Mechanism**:
  - Introduce a philosophical framing question.
  - Explain the technology through a familiar metaphor, then connect it to human learning.
  - Compare artistic practice, commercial remakes, and formulaic production.
  - Expand from the tools to the incentives of companies and the possible effects on workers.
  - Return to the difference between fast output and personal experience or human judgement.
- **Source Observation**:
  - The excerpt names thinkers, artists, and films but does not show a consistent spoken citation pattern for those references. Additional verification and visual sourcing are required.
- **Core Learning**:
  - A philosophical question can hold the whole video together, but examples should build a reasoned argument rather than merely accumulate references. Mark job-displacement forecasts as forecasts, not certain outcomes.

### Sample 4: `science_space`
- **Observed Hook**: A universal question about intelligent life introduces a real space mission carrying a message, creating both wonder and a concrete investigation target.
- **Hook Mechanism**:
  - Start with a question viewers can understand without specialist knowledge.
  - Attach the question to a real mission, instrument, or discovery.
  - Reveal the unusual detail that the rest of the video will explain.
- **Body Mechanism**:
  - Give the mission's original purpose and historical context.
  - Explain why the route, instrument, or energy source works.
  - Follow discoveries chronologically and define technical terms where needed.
  - Use diagrams to make invisible boundaries and trajectories visible.
  - Explain what observations support and avoid implying that a mission proves a broader idea it did not test.
- **Source Observation**:
  - The excerpt names NASA and gives dates and mission figures, but does not regularly name a specific mission document or scientific publication aloud. Mission records and current authoritative data must appear in the output source ledger.
- **Core Learning**:
  - Wonder is the hook; the mechanism is the payoff. Current numeric facts should be checked against up-to-date mission and scientific sources before writing.

## 4. Genre-Aware Hook Engine (`hookEngine`)

| Genre | Choose When | Structural Pattern | Avoid |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **mystery / paranormal** | Strange report, folklore, puzzling event, or widely shared unexplained claim | Specific claimed experience -> why people believe it -> what can be verified -> central mystery question | Declaring supernatural causation before considering ordinary explanations |
| **investigation / public accountability** | Dated event, document, chain of decisions, numerical discrepancy, or affected citizens | Timestamp or document -> verified contradiction -> real-world stakes -> who/what must be investigated | Treating suspicious coincidence or an accusation as proof of guilt |
| **science / space** | Real mechanism, mission, or observation answers a big question | Universal question -> specific mission or observation -> surprising implication -> scientific question | Overstating what the experiment or mission establishes |
| **AI / technology / culture** | Technology changes a familiar process or creates an ethical, creative, or labour question | Concrete capability -> apparent contradiction -> human consequence -> mechanism to explain | Treating a demo as proof of universal capability or a prediction as a fact |
| **history / biography** | Turning point, historical document, or contradiction in the popular story | Decisive moment or document -> public assumption -> unresolved historical question -> contextual rewind | Inventing dialogue or private thoughts without marking them as reconstruction |
| **disaster / survival** | Verified timeline and physical mechanism explain how an event unfolded | Time and place -> ordinary situation -> overlooked risk -> minute-by-minute question | Using unverified exact timestamps or sensationalising victims |

## 5. Source Placement Protocol (`sourcePlacementProtocol`)

- **Core Principle**: Place evidence beside the claim it supports. A bibliography at the end does not cure a misleading attribution in the narration.
- **Recommended Cadence**:
  - **Critical or Disputed Claim**: Show the source on screen with the claim, and name the source in narration at the claim or in the next sentence when natural.
  - **Dense Factual Section**: Add a meaningful source anchor or document cue about every 120–180 spoken words, or more often when new claims require it.
  - **Timeline or Case Study**: Attach a source to every consequential hand-off, date, payment, official action, or allegation.
  - **Science or Health**: Cite the relevant study, agency, mission record, or scientific consensus near the claim; distinguish measured result from interpretation.
  - **Short-Form**: Use concise, readable on-screen source labels at the claim and place complete references in the description or pinned source list.
- **Minimum Source Ledger Fields**:
  `claimId`, `exactClaim`, `sourceTitle`, `publisherOrInstitution`, `authorIfAvailable`, `publicationDateOrRecordDate`, `directUrl`, `sourceType`, `whatTheSourceSupports`, `limitationsOrContradictoryEvidence`, `spokenAtApproximateTime`, `visualCueAtApproximateTime`.

## 6. Language Engine (`languageEngine`) & Promotion Engine (`promotionEngine`)

### Language Modes
- **English (`english`)**: Clear English with common words, short clauses, and explicit definitions.
- **Roman Hinglish (`hinglishRoman`)**: Conversational Hindi/Urdu grammar in Latin script with English technical terms where they are clearer. Never mix Devanagari characters into Roman Hinglish.
- **Urdu Script (`urduScript`)**: Natural spoken Urdu with standard English technical terms when they improve clarity.

### Promotion Engine
- **Default State**: `enabledByDefault: false`.
- **Activation Rule**: Only write a promotional segment when `promotion.enabled === true`. If false or absent, omit all promotional copy and CTAs.
- **Copy Guardrails**: Never invent price, discount, deadline, scarcity, lesson counts, testimonials, learner counts, or guarantees. Return to the main documentary after the pitch rather than ending inside the advertisement.
